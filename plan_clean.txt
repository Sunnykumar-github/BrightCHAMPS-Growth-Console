1. Executive summary
The situation
BrightChamps is doubling down on one market and one subject: the USA and Math. Two things must improve every month: the quantity of leads and the quality of leads. Today the blended cost per lead (CPL) is $50. The goal is $25, which means the same media budget must buy roughly twice as many leads, or the same number of leads must cost half as much.
The thesis
CPL is not one number to push on. It is the output of three levers, each owned by a different team:
CPL = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × Landing-page CVR)
CPM (cost to be seen) and CTR (share of viewers who click) are the marketing engine.
Landing-page CVR (share of clicks that become a lead with a booked trial) is the product engine.
A third lever, audience expansion, changes the blend of who we buy attention from, and is the only lever expected to lift quality as well as lower cost.

Every plan value sits below the published 2025 benchmark median for its lever (Meta CTR median about 2.19%, education landing-page conversion median about 5.7%). The plan does not need heroic execution on any one lever. It needs three ordinary improvements to land together.
The guardrail
Halving CPL is easy if quality is allowed to fall: loosen targeting, buy junk traffic, and $25 arrives while the business quietly breaks. Every recommendation here therefore travels with a lead-quality index that must stay at or above 100 (baseline). A relative drop of more than 15% in lead-to-paid conversion pauses the responsible lever. The mathematical limit is generous, because halving CPL leaves room for quality to fall by up to 50% before cost per paid student worsens, but the plan uses a far tighter tripwire on purpose (Section 8).
What success looks like
Month 1: CPL about $43 from quick wins (form cut, creative refresh). Month 3: about $30. Month 5: $25. Month 12: about $20 with audience expansion working (commit $25, stretch $20).
At constant $100K monthly media budget: leads rise from about 2,000 to about 4,000 per month. At constant lead volume: $50K per month of media budget is released.
Cost per paid student (media only, illustrative funnel) falls from about $712 to about $356 if lead quality holds.
Decisions requested
Approve a Week 0 measurement audit so illustrative baselines are replaced with account actuals.
Approve landing-page and booking-flow capacity (design, front-end, analytics) for a rolling experiment program.
Ring-fence an 8% test reserve of media budget for new formats and audience segments.
Adopt the guardrail and kill rules in Sections 8 and 14 as standing policy.
Name a single owner per engine, and a weekly review with authority to reallocate budget.
Honest scope note
No account data was available for this plan. Baselines are calibrated to reproduce the stated $50 CPL using published 2025 platform benchmarks. Treat every figure as a hypothesis to be confirmed or replaced in the first 14 days. The structure of the plan, the guardrails and the decision rules do not depend on the exact baseline.

3. The unit-economics model
3.1 From impressions to leads
For any spend S, impressions = S ÷ CPM × 1,000, clicks = impressions × CTR, and leads = clicks × CVR. Dividing spend by leads gives CPL = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × CVR). CPL falls when CPM falls, and it falls just as much when CTR or CVR rises by the same proportion. That symmetry is why the plan pulls both engines together rather than betting on one.

4.3 Capacity check before choosing Option A
Twice the leads means about twice the trial classes, twice the reminders and twice the follow-up calls. Before scaling volume, answer four questions: How many trial slots can teachers deliver per week in each US time zone? How many leads can one representative contact within five minutes and within one hour? What is the no-show recovery process? What is the cost of a trial class (teacher time)? If any answer says capacity is fixed, choose Option C and reinvest released budget in quality improvements, not volume.

6.4 Speed, mobile and technical hygiene
Largest contentful paint under 2.5 seconds on mid-range phones. Compress images, defer scripts, remove pop-ups. Most paid social traffic is mobile.
One-thumb forms: numeric keypad for phone, autofill for name and email, inline error messages, no captcha unless bot traffic is proven.
Message match: one page variant per ad group, with dynamic headline text where needed, so the promise in the ad is the first thing seen on the page.
Human fallback: click-to-call or text option for parents who prefer to talk, subject to consent rules.
Event tracking: page view, form start, step completion, slot selection, booking confirmation, each with the ad and creative identifier.

7. Audience expansion
7.1 Why expand, and why now
A single broad audience saturates: frequency rises, CTR falls and CPM climbs. Expansion audiences add fresh inventory, and some carry higher affinity for structured supplemental math. Two anchors from public data (approximate, US Census and Pew Research Center):
Indian-Americans: about 5.2 to 5.5 million people, with the highest median household income of any major US ethnic group (about $145,000 to $151,000). Small, high-fit and concentrated in a few metros.
Hispanic and Latino population: about 62 to 63 million (about 19% of the US), with a young age profile and the fastest-growing under-18 cohort. Large and currently thin in premium math edtech advertising.
Population size is not the same as addressable demand. Household income, grade-age children, digital reach and willingness to pay for live online tutoring shrink the number quickly. That is why the plan tests before it commits.

8. Lead quality and the guardrail
8.1 The lead-quality index
Quality is defined by what happens after the lead, not by how the lead looks. The index compares three downstream rates to their own pre-launch baselines (rolling 90 days before launch):
Quality index = 100 x [ 0.30 x (booked ÷ booked₀) + 0.30 x (show ÷ show₀) + 0.40 x (paid ÷ paid₀) ]
Worked example: booked 58% vs. 60%, show 66% vs. 65%, paid 17% vs. 18% gives an index of 97.2, which sits in the amber band.
Timing: paid conversion lags about four weeks. For the first four weeks of any cohort use 50% booked and 50% show; add paid when the cohort matures.

8.3 The CAC-neutral quality threshold
Halving CPL creates room for quality to fall before the business is worse off. Using the illustrative funnel (7.02% of leads become paid students), baseline media cost per paid student is about $712. If CPL falls to $25 but the lead-to-paid rate falls by a fraction d, cost per paid student is $25 ÷ (0.0702 x (1 - d)). It only matches baseline when d reaches 50%. At $30 CPL the break-even decline is 40%, and at $35 it is 30%.

8.6 The handoff to sales and onboarding
Lead quality is a joint result of marketing and the team that calls the lead. A 5-minute response rule is meaningless if the queue is longer than the day.
Capacity formula: representatives needed = daily leads x average contact minutes per lead ÷ productive minutes per representative per day. At 4,000 leads per month (about 133 per business day), with 6 contact minutes per lead and 300 productive minutes per representative, about 3 representatives are needed for first contact alone, before follow-up.
Tag every unqualified lead with a reason (wrong grade, not interested, unreachable, price). Review reasons weekly with marketing. This is the fastest source of targeting and creative fixes.
Share one dashboard. Marketing sees show and paid rates by campaign; sales sees CPL and lead scores by campaign. Neither team optimizes in the dark.

Forty-five metrics sounds like too many. It is not, if each has an owner, a cadence and a threshold that triggers action. Metrics are grouped so that a weekly review can read a handful of headline numbers and drill into a group only when a headline moves. Baselines marked TBD are set in Week 0.
11.1 Metric tree
How CPL decomposes
CPL = CPM ÷ (1,000 x CTR x CVR). CPM and CTR belong to the marketing engine. CVR belongs to the product engine.
CVR = form start rate x step completion rate x slot booking rate. Each step has its own owner-level metric.
Quality = booked rate x show rate x paid rate. Show rate and paid rate belong jointly to product, sales and teaching operations.

Appendix C. Sources and benchmark notes
Figures are approximate, drawn from public sources in 2025 to 2026, and should be re-verified before use in any external document.
Meta advertising benchmarks (2025): platform-wide medians for CPM (about $13 to $14 overall, up to about $20.70 in competitive verticals) and CTR (about 2.19%) as reported in benchmark analyses by Triple Whale and Influee. These skew toward e-commerce accounts.
Landing-page conversion benchmarks: Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report (lead-generation average about 11.9%; education median about 5.7%; all-industry averages roughly 3 to 4%).
Indian-American population and income: Pew Research Center analyses of US Census data (about 5.2 to 5.5 million people; median household income about $145,000 to $151,000).
Hispanic and Latino population and growth: US Census Bureau estimates, with purchasing-power and growth analyses from Claritas and MarketingCharts (about 62 to 63 million, roughly 19% of the US population).
Platform guidance: Google Stitch, Meta and Google advertising policies should be re-read at launch, because rules on targeting, education claims and minors change frequently.
